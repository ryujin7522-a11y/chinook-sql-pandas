# Chinook Warm-up Project

SQL → pandas pipeline practice using the Chinook sample database (digital music store).

## Setup
- SQLite database, queried via `sqlite3` + `pandas.read_sql_query`
- Charts via matplotlib

## Questions & Findings

**1. Top 10 customers by total spend**
Helena is the number one customer in terms of total spend. However, the difference in spending between her and Victor (tenth highest spender) is negligible at 7 dollars. This could suggest that the highest-spending customers, who tend to be frequent shoppers due to the low per-item prices and are therefore loyal customers, all have similar lifetime value. 

**2. Which genres bring in the most revenue**
Rock dominates in terms of total revenue by genre, with margins in revenue being over double between rock and Latin, the genre bringing in the 2nd most revenues. Additionally, the total revenue generated by rock is almost double that of the total spending by the top 10 customers, which suggests that rock is a popular genre throughout the entire customer base rather than a genre concentrated at the top. 

**3. Monthly revenue trend**
Over a trailing 5 year period, revenue is remarkably steady around a stable baseline of about 37 dollars, with occassional one to three month long spikes and dips that ultimately return to the baseline. This is further demonstrated by the rolling average, which further smooths out these seemingly dramatic changes, showing that changes in revenue is mostly noise.

**4. Revenue by country**
North America represents the largest single concentration of revenue, representing about 35.5% of total revenue, though the remaining majority is spread across the other 22 countries.

**5. Customers who've bought from every media type**
Exactly one customer (Bjørn Hansen) has purchased from all 5 media types in the store.

## Notes / errors hit along the way
- Window function `OVER (ORDER BY ...)` can't reference a column alias defined in the same SELECT
- Grouping date strings needs year-first formatting (`%Y-%m`, not `%m-%Y`) or `ORDER BY` sorts alphabetically instead of chronologically
- `HAVING count = 5` works but is unreliable, which is why I swapped it for a subquery (`= (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM MediaType)`) so it stays correct if the table changes
- Pandas `.rolling(3).mean()` reproduces the same idea as a SQL window frame, just in the another layer
